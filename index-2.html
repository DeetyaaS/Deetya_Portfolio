About me

My values

  • Teamwork

    I love taking a real problem from sketch to SolidWorks to a machined, tested part, but what I value most is building it with a team. The best ideas come from people with different strengths working toward the same goal.

  • Family & friends

    My family and friends are my biggest supporters, and my everyday interactions with the people around me are what keep me going. I make a point to invest in those connections and show up for the people I care about.

  • Service

    I believe in leaving every space I enter better than I found it, whether that means mentoring middle schoolers, coaching all-girl robotics teams, or tutoring math.

  • Always learning

    I say yes to new experiences and new skills, especially the ones outside my comfort zone. Every one of them makes me a better engineer and a better teammate.

  • Engineering that helps

    I want my engineering skills to make a difference, whether that’s for society, the people around me, our coral reefs, or endangered species.

My story

  1. Curious from the start

    I was born in Chicago and moved to Los Angeles in second grade. Some of my best childhood memories are from museums, aquariums, and planetariums, and between those trips and a lot of PBS Kids, I grew up curious about how the world around me works. That’s where my love for learning started.

  2. Stumbling into robotics

    On a trip to Legoland, my curiosity led me to wander into a FIRST Lego League competition. I joined FLL in third grade, went on to FIRST Robotics in high school, and now build underwater robots for RoboSub at Berkeley. I can’t imagine my life without robotics and engineering.

  3. Falling for the ocean

    My family comes from a coastal town in India, so I’ve always felt connected to the water. Countless hours at the Shedd Aquarium and the Aquarium of the Pacific made me want to learn everything I could about the ocean, and learning about the threats facing my happy place led me to volunteer at the Aquarium. Today I’m a certified scuba diver, and some of my favorite projects started in the water.

  4. Finding my why

    While helping lead the Teen Science Cafe’s “Women in Antarctica” series, I met scientists who showed me the engineering that makes research possible in places as extreme as Antarctica. That’s when it clicked: I could use my engineering skills to help the causes I care about. That drive comes from my experiences and the people in my life, which is why I always make time for them.

Solo projectUC Berkeley · Jan–May 2026

Wave Energy Collector

Solo Designer and Engineer

A small-scale wave energy harvester that turns ocean wave heave into electricity, using a rack-and-pinion mechanism coupled to a permanent magnet alternator, designed to be modular and deployable in the field.

Built to power remote ocean sensing and monitoring equipment at sea. Every design decision came from real ocean conditions: daily direction reversals, wave-induced uplift and lateral loading, subsystem alignment under load, and the need for waterproof, modular systems that can be serviced in the field.

  • Designed a two-DOF drive system with rubber-bushed rod guides and chamfered gear teeth to handle the stress of daily direction reversals from wave oscillation.
  • Engineered a catenary mooring with a spiked anchor plate to resist wave-induced uplift and lateral loading.
  • Decoupled the mechanical and electrical subsystems for modularity, and constrained float motion to keep the rack-pinion pitch line aligned under realistic wave loading.
  • Fully waterproofed every system and designed for straightforward field servicing.
  • SolidWorks
  • Ocean Engineering
  • Energy Harvesting
  • Waterproofing
  • Mooring Design

Small-Scale Wind Turbine

Design Team Member, 4-Person Team

Designed, 3D printed, and tested a small-scale wind turbine that produced a peak of 454 mW at 25 mph on a 244 g printed tower.

  • Rotor: Three-blade NACA 4412 design with a 6° angle of attack and a 12° to 2° twist, chosen for its higher lift-to-drag ratio.

Coral Reef Bleaching Prediction System

Independent Researcher

Corrected systematic errors in NASA satellite sea surface temperature data using my own field measurements, then trained a machine learning model on the corrected data to predict where coral bleaching is likely next.

Satellite data for reef monitoring had systematic discrepancies that made bleaching predictions unreliable. I dove 14 sites across the Pacific to collect in-situ turbidity and temperature measurements, used them to find and correct those errors, and trained a predictive model on the cleaner dataset to flag the reefs most at risk.

  • Reconciled in-situ data from 14 reef sites against NASA Landsat-8 satellite data, improving bleaching prediction accuracy by 31% and reducing false-positive alerts.
  • Trained a Random Forest model on the corrected dataset, spanning 8 years and 3 ocean regions, for 4x faster risk assessment and a 27% improvement in predictive precision.
